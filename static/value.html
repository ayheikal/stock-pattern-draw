Fair value is what the stock would cost at the median multiple of its peers (its industry when 3+ companies there report the figure, else its sector): its earnings at the peers' P/E, its book value at their P/B and, except for banks and other lenders, its operating profit at their EV/EBITDA. The fair price is the median of these (at least two), and no single one can put it more than 3× above or below the price.
Cheap isn't the same as a chance to buy: a low multiple often means the market expects worse. So each stock also gets the fundamental scorecard and its long-term trend. Buy zone = cheap, a sound business and above its 200-day average. Watch for a turn = cheap and sound but still falling. Cheap for a reason = losing money or weak fundamentals. This is relative value: if a whole sector is overpriced, its cheapest member can still be dear, and last year's profit may include one-offs. Open the chart and the buy checklist before buying.
